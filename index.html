Enter Gretchen: a young witch with magnificent white robes, a hat that tilts slightly to one side, and magic that is almost certainly under control. Together they fly by broomstick to a faraway land called Vaelmere — a place of ancient forests, dancing northern lights, and creatures made entirely of living light.

When Morgan discovers a small glimwick who has lost its glow, she must find a way to help it. Not with spells. Not with wands. Just with a very small hand, held very still, and the simple courage of being kind.

The World of Vaelmere

Sovereigns of the Shattered Veil

The bedtime stories are real. They were always real.

A World of Its Own

A World Complete

Vaelmere is a world entire — not a reflection of our own, not a space between other places. It has its own deep history, its own ancient inhabitants who long predate any human presence within it, its own divine order, and its own long road.

Humans were brought to Vaelmere as something more than settlers: as a catalyst. The Ancient Race who already inhabited it were magnificent — long-lived, powerful, deeply connected to the living world. But they had reached a point of such sufficiency that nothing new was entering their world. What humans carried with them was exactly what the Ancient Race had forgotten they needed: mortality. The specific ferocity of love from creatures who know that everything is temporary. The reaching, the incompleteness, the refusal to stop. The real command underneath the command was to let the humans teach them what they’d forgotten they didn’t know. That encounter failed catastrophically. The wound has been open for a thousand years.

A single continent runs north to south, with a chain of mountains forming its western spine. An island realm lies to the southeast. Beyond the western mountains: the Dark Lands. Beyond the veil that separates Vaelmere from our world: a four-year-old girl who doesn’t yet know she is a princess.

The Divine Order

Seraphina oversees the realms — immortal, angelic, of a nature that exceeds any simple category. She is not God. She sits below beings she can feel the weight of but not fully see. Below her: six bloodlines she chose long ago. Beside her, in a category of her own: a great white wolf who answers to no one.

Magic

Magic is real in all realms of Vaelmere. The ability to wield it is rare. Most of the princesses cannot use it at all. Even those with natural aptitude find it difficult to master — as Gretchen’s many spectacular miscasts demonstrate daily. The moment it becomes easy or routine, the stakes vanish.

On the Title “Princess”

The rulers of Vaelmere’s kingdoms are sovereign in every practical sense. By any conventional definition they are queens. They call themselves princesses — an act of cultural deference to Seraphina and to what lies above her. To call yourself queen in Vaelmere is to declare yourself the final authority. The six have always known they are not.

The Collapse of the Bridge
Earth — Without the Bridge

When the portals collapsed, the branch of humanity on Earth was cut off entirely. No magic. No sacred bloodlines to tend. No bridge back. What developed in its place was built from nothing but restless, incomplete, brilliant minds — cities visible from the stars, medicine that would look like sorcery in Vaelmere, eight billion people connected by will alone. Earth’s intellect was not a gift. It was necessity, made magnificent.

Vaelmere — The Soul

Vaelmere kept the sacred bloodlines — the deep connection to the living world, magic drawn from the fiber of existence, wisdom accumulating across centuries in the six royal houses. It kept what Earth lost. But without the bridge, without the catalytic presence of Earth humanity’s restless incompleteness, Vaelmere has been fighting the same war for a thousand years without resolution.

The Seventh Bloodline — a sacred Vaelmere line — went to Earth when the bridge collapsed and matured there across a thousand ordinary years, shaped by mortality without magic. In Morgan, that bloodline surfaces fully. In Sylvie, the Vaelmere soul reaches its highest distillation. Together they are the proxies the reunion requires — the same sacred tradition expressed through two different conditions, choosing wholeness simultaneously. A complete humanity doesn’t defeat the darkness. It renders it irrelevant.

The Calendar

All dates in Vaelmere’s recorded history are given in AB — After the Banishment. The founding event of the world’s recorded history was not the building of its kingdoms but the exile of the race that preceded them. The story opens in 980 AB. The millennium falls in 1000 AB, twenty years later — when Morgan is twenty-four years old, and whatever has been building since Year 0 arrives at last.

Guardian of All

Seraphina

Immortal and angelic, of an order of being that predates the oldest of the realms. She appears to be somewhere in her forties. Dark warm brown hair. Eyes that are blue, edging toward violet, carrying a quiet that is not quite stillness. She wears flowing pink gowns. Her wings appear only occasionally, in fleeting translucent glimpses. What is always present is a subtle warmth that marks her, unmistakably, as something other than human.

She intervenes directly only when circumstances exceed what the princesses and their familiars can handle alone. A protector who solves every problem produces people who cannot face any. When she appears in full — and especially in fierce form, in black, carrying a silver and gold sword — the room goes quiet. That silence is earned by her absence everywhere else.

The Sovereigns

The Six Sovereigns

They were chosen before they were born. This is what they became.

The Rimefell

Astrid

Princess-Sovereign · House Veldric

Proud, disciplined, fierce, and slow to trust. A woman of deep feeling who has become expert at not letting those feelings lead. Perhaps the most physically formidable of the six — a gifted swordswoman who personally patrols her own realm with her men. Daughter of two legends, she has carved out her own space with her people as Astrid the Good. Of all the mortal sovereigns, she comes closest to embodying Seraphina’s own duality.

Auvenmere

Maren

Princess-Sovereign · House Merevin

An intelligence of the highest order — genuinely, structurally brilliant in a way that operates at the level of the oldest and most formidable beings in the world. The fellowship’s scholar and its conscience, gifted with magical sensory capabilities as an empath. She ascended at sixteen in grief, without preparation. She has governed Auvenmere since that morning with a competence that looks effortless from the outside. She marvels at her daughter who at three already seems destined for greatness beyond her own — and wonders, as the world rushes toward a millennial crisis, whether that will prove a blessing or a curse.

The Eldenmere

Elowen

Princess-Sovereign · House Avalorn

At seventeen, the youngest sovereign ruling the oldest realm in Vaelmere. Auburn hair. Emerald eyes. Freckles. Heart as large as Maren’s. Comfortably tradition-bound in the human sense and wildly adventurous in the natural one — deeply attuned to the creatures of the Old Forest in a way that seems less like animal affinity and more like a prior arrangement.

Al-Sahra

Zahra

Princess-Sovereign · House Zahari

Sharp, warm, and vibrant. A woman whose grace commands admiration, whose beauty is only exceeded by her virtue, whose presence radiates royal dignity. The Desert Pearl has reigned magnificently, vastly improving Al-Sahra’s prestige among the six kingdoms. The most mesmerizing of Vaelmere’s sovereigns — she immediately owns any room she enters.

The Golden Shore

Calliope

Princess-Sovereign · House Callivane

Golden blonde. A register of genuine warmth and extravagance that are not performance and substance but the same thing expressed simultaneously. A capable sovereign whose grandiosity occasionally overshoots her judgment. She loves enormously and acts from that love without always asking what it costs. The Golden Shore’s most recent history has tested her in ways its sunny exterior did not prepare her for.

Tavakai

Naia

Princess-Sovereign · House Marakai

Bold, direct, and occasionally oblivious to social nuance in a way that generates both comedy and genuine warmth. She comes from furthest away — an outsider to the continent and its long memories. She hears the ocean in ways that may eventually become something more. Her outsider clarity is its own kind of gift.
Beyond the Throne

Principal Characters

Not all of the significant figures in Vaelmere wear a crown.

The Seventh Princess

Morgan

The Princess of Our World. Four years old.

She lives in our world, in a real town, with a real best friend and a real bedtime she sometimes resists. She insists that her stuffed snowman is real, too. She did not know, at the beginning of the first book, that she was a princess. She discovered this when Seraphina appeared in her room one night. The discovery was not the end of anything. It was the beginning of everything.

Morgan carries the Seventh Bloodline — a sacred Vaelmere line stranded on Earth when the portals collapsed almost a thousand years ago. It diluted across centuries of ordinary human lives, becoming functionally invisible. Almost lost. In Morgan it surfaces fully — not gradually, not partially. Completely. As if it was always going to be her, and the intervening centuries were simply the long way around.

She carries Vaelmere blood shaped by an Earth life — the sacred tradition matured in the one context that could prepare it for what the millennium requires. The Gathering has always had an empty chair. Seraphina presides from her own throne facing all seven; she does not occupy any of them. The seventh has simply been empty since the portals collapsed. It belongs to Morgan.

Guardian Witch

Gretchen

Morgan’s guardian. Born Auvenmere. Converted on Reaping Night, 979 AB.

Long silver-white hair. White robes. A hat that tilts slightly to one side. Magic that is almost certainly under control.

Before the white: born into a wealthy Auvenmere family — not nobility, but close enough to see the door and never be admitted. Smarter than most of them. More magically gifted than almost all. The wound that followed calcified into something darker. She spent ten years in the Dark Lands under another name, becoming the most gifted dark witch in generations — and a terrorist feared and despised across all of Vaelmere.

After surviving a harrowing conversion ritual performed by Seraphina herself, she has been assigned as guardian to an extraordinary child from another world. Seraphina told her the new job will challenge her more than anything she has ever done, and heal her in ways that Seraphina cannot. Thus far, both have proven to be true.

The Darkness

The Count

His name is Lucian. He abandoned it three centuries ago.

A vampire of extraordinary age, sophistication, and power. He does not age. He appears to be in his late thirties — old enough to carry gravitas, young enough that you can still see the nobleman he was before he stumbled into an eternal curse. The most powerful creature in the Dark Lands, and the most feared in Vaelmere.

After he was cursed, he spent two centuries committing unspeakable atrocities across the Dark Lands and the Six Realms. More recently he has grown melancholy and bored with his own evil, and has begun seeking out mortals who interest him — including an unlikely friendship with one of the Princess-Sovereigns.

Most recently, a four-year-old girl looked into the shadows at the edge of a bedroom window and said: I see you. I’ve always seen you. He has not been the same since.

Realms Commander of the Six Crowns · Maren’s Husband

Aldric

Maren’s beloved. Holder of the Realms Commander position.

Common-born with uncommon bravery and honor. Former special forces commando, former commanding general of Auvenmere forces, current Realms Commander of the Six Crowns. He exists in an enchanted form — the permanent consequence of a mission that went catastrophically wrong and a curse designed specifically to be outside Seraphina’s power to reverse.

He was largely restored. The fearsome aspect in his features, voice, and bearing never fully left. It has enhanced rather than diminished his effectiveness as Realms Commander. Maren, who knows the man inside it, does not require the conventional version of him. She never did.

Governor-General · Marisange

Celestine

Governor-General and Viceroy of Marisange. Reports to Princess-Sovereign Maren.

Elite vampire hunter said to have been trained by Seraphina herself — a claim she has neither confirmed nor denied. She runs toward the darkness instead of away from it and has done so for years with considerable reputation.

She and the Count know each other very well. He visits Marisange semi-regularly to engage in good-natured taunting. Both are clear about who would win a fight. He admires her courage anyway.

The Great White Wolf

Blizzard

Exists between worlds. Answers to no one.

Known as “the Wolf” or “the White Wolf” to all except Morgan, who calls her Blizzard. She appears when she chooses and leaves when she chooses. At home in our ordinary world and in the magical realms alike — which should be impossible and is.

She saved Morgan from an escaped lion at a zoo in a spectacular intervention before the divine hierarchy had even registered Morgan’s existence. She was present in Vaelmere before Seraphina knew to look. Her purpose is entirely her own. Her identity is completely unknown.

She appears to be benevolent. Her presence unsettles Seraphina anyway. And it terrifies the Count.

Elowen’s Familiar · Ancient Race

Aelindra

One of the oldest beings in Vaelmere. Converted by her own will, without help.

Ancient Race. She was barely a woman when the transgression occurred — young enough to be innocent of it personally, old enough to inherit its full weight. She did not choose the fall. She chose, entirely alone and without Seraphina’s hand, to return from it. She showed herself a possibility. She made a choice. She is fully realized in her new nature in a way Gretchen is still working toward.

Formidable, arch, capable of genuine warmth expressed entirely on her own terms. She chose Elowen, whom she found sleeping in an ancient forest, and never left. She and the Count speak with the precision of beings who know each other from before everything that currently defines them.

Also of the Court
Al-Sahra

Zafar

Prince of Al-Sahra. Military commander.

When the realm calls, he answers precisely and without theater. He asks few questions in rooms where questions are unwelcome, and exactly the right one when they are. He appeared in Book Three with a stolen-monkey problem that turned out to be considerably more serious than it looked.

The Golden Shore

Cassandra

Heiress apparent. House Callivane.

Calliope’s daughter. Eighteen and warm and magnetic in the way of her mother — she fills rooms before she enters them. Her coronation is imminent. Those close to her have noticed some odd behaviours of late that nobody has quite found the words for. She is carrying something. What it is, she has not said.

The Golden Shore

Lyssene

Callivane y Richelieu · Twenty-third in line.

A distant branch of House Callivane, living an ordinary life on a ranch in the far north near the Auvenmere border. Twenty-third in the line of succession, which has always felt entirely theoretical. She mentioned it once and nobody cared, and that was fine with her. She is fifteen years old.

The Golden Shore

Rodrigo

Vizconde Callivane y Vidal. Lyssene’s father.

First cousin to Princess-Sovereign Calliope, with full access to Solanthis and its courts — none of which he has used in years. He chose a ranch and a quiet life over everything his bloodline entitled him to, and built it deliberately. He has raised Lyssene alone since she was eight, when her mother Celine died. He is not a man who makes that kind of choice without knowing what he is giving up.

The Rimefell

Kaia

Heiress Presumptive · House Veldric

Equally beautiful and, those who know both women will tell you, considerably more approachable. Beloved in the Rimefell for a gentle and cheerful nature that has nothing to do with the throne — and never has. She is recently engaged and looking forward, with undisguised relief, to the day she stops being a heartbeat from succession. If only her sister would produce an heir.

Heiress Apparent

Maren and Aldric’s daughter. She has her mother’s mind and her father’s steadiness, which is either a remarkable inheritance or a considerable weight for one person to carry. Probably both.

Maren and Aldric’s son. Young enough that the weight of Auvenmere has not yet landed on him, old enough to be watching everything. The candlelight finds his face and he is already paying attention.

Heiress Presumptive · Zafar’s daughter

She grew up in the desert and the court in equal measure. Her grandfather’s scholarship and her father’s warmth arrived in her simultaneously. Al-Sahra is watching her with quiet confidence.

Heiress Apparent

Bright of spirit and fierce with joy. She loves the sea above all, runs into the surf before anyone else is awake, and has been known to navigate by stars she learned before she learned her letters.

Curious, creative, and quick to learn. She builds sandcastles the way her people build canoes — with intention, with patience, with an eye for what will hold. She builds beauty in her own way.

After the Banishment

A History of Vaelmere

The calendar begins with a banishment. It ends, perhaps, with a door.

Year 0 AB

The Banishment

The Ancient Race — once luminous, once adjacent to the divine, the original guardians of what would become The Eldenmere — are exiled to the Dark Lands. Vaelmere’s calendar begins here. What it calls history is everything that came after.

~680 AB

The Forest Path

Lucian of House Avalorn finds a chest on his estates in The Eldenmere. Inside, beneath jewels, a lamp. He rubs the lamp. A match of wits follows. He loses. He is turned into a vampire. Walking back to his estate, a preternaturally beautiful woman appears beside him on the path and offers him the exit. He laughs at her. The Count begins. The woman was Seraphina. She has been watching him ever since.

~955–962 AB

The Second Witching War

A period of dark-forces insurgency across the northern realms. Bjorn, Commander of all Rimefell forces — a war hero, the template for what a man can be — is treacherously murdered at a staged truce meeting. His daughter Kira is seven years old. She has known the truth for twenty years. The wound shapes everything that follows.

~970 AB

Sylvaine’s Funeral

Sylvaine of Auvenmere dies unexpectedly. Her daughter Maren ascends at sixteen — no regency, no buffer, in grief. At the state funeral, Kira steps out of the formal line and takes Maren’s hand. They walk the rest of the procession together. The people of Auvenmere never forget it. Kira is never called the ice queen in Auvenmere again.

973–975 AB

Aldric’s Curse

An Ancient Race enchantress curses Aldric — a targeted attack on House Merevin, designed to prevent what she understood was coming. Maren approaches the Count with two requests. The enchantress is destroyed. A white wolf is briefly glimpsed at the treeline and gone. Aldric is largely restored, though the fearsome aspect in his features never fully leaves.

979 AB — Reaping Night

Gretchen’s Conversion

In the mountain pass between the Dark Lands and Auvenmere, Seraphina intercepts Gretchen and converts her at enormous personal cost. The conversion is real. Gretchen almost does not survive it. Blizzard watches from the ridge above. The fellowship is changed before it knows it has been.

980 AB

The Story Opens

Gretchen’s Homecoming. The board is set. In our world, a four-year-old girl named Morgan receives a guardian she did not ask for, and everything begins.

~1000 AB — The Millennium

The Shattered Veil

The millennium falls twenty years after the story opens. The Banishment strains. The Ancient Race stirs in the Dark Lands. The veil between worlds approaches its crisis. Morgan is twenty-four years old. Whatever she has been carrying since a night in an ordinary bedroom has been building toward this. The Count has known it was coming. He is the only one who checked his arithmetic.
Beyond the Western Mountains

The Darkness

The bedtime stories are only the beginning. This is what they translate.

The Territory

The Dark Lands

Beyond the western mountains, the world becomes something else. The borders of the Dark Lands are undefined and boundless — darkness, appropriately, has no neat edges.

The Dark Lands are not called the Dark Lands because dark things always lived there. Dark things live there because a luminous race slowly lost their light in the centuries after their banishment. The darkness here is not the original nature of the place. It is what happens to anything that lives long enough without light.

Before the Kingdoms

The Ancient Race & First Blood

Before Vaelmere had kingdoms, there was a race of beings whose homeland was the forest at what would become The Eldenmere. Long-lived, powerful, deeply connected to the living world in ways that have no equivalent in any current culture. Originally luminous. Originally adjacent to the divine. And — by the time humans arrived in Vaelmere — magnificently, dangerously static. They had reached a point of such sufficiency that nothing new was entering their world.

Humans were brought to Vaelmere as a catalyst for exactly this. What humans carried — mortality, loss, love with the full weight of impermanence behind it — was what the Ancient Race had forgotten they needed. The command underneath the command was to let the humans teach them what they’d forgotten they didn’t know.

First Blood was not the Ancient Race’s worst instinct surfacing on its own. It was the Being’s agenda executing through them. The specific pride it had cultivated in them — their sense of sufficiency, completeness, superiority — was weaponized against the humans they were commanded to welcome. A transformed Ancient Race, genuinely changed by relationship with humanity, would have been completely outside the Being’s influence. The genocide was the Being protecting its investment.

The Banishment followed in Year 0 AB. The Ancient Race were exiled to the Dark Lands — not destroyed, removed. The Being was not banished with them. It was imprisoned separately, in the lamp, because it was the source. Banishment was insufficient for what it is.

The Banishment was always a term with conditions — not a permanent sentence. A thousand years was the sentence. The millennium is the moment it reaches its natural end. What happens then depends entirely on whether the wound has healed.

The Ancient Darkness

The Count

His name is Lucian. He abandoned it three centuries ago. Born into the extended nobility of House Avalorn — a genuine Avalorn bloodline, a count’s title earned by land and standing. He found a chest on his estates in The Eldenmere. A match of wits with the being in the lamp. He lost.

He was born into a rigid aristocratic system and formed by it before he became what he is. That formation did not disappear with the vampirism. It calcified. Broad, impersonal villainy is beneath him — not morally, but aesthetically. Vulgar. He engages with individuals he finds worthy of his specific attention.

Seraphina is the only being who uses his birth name — Lucian — and only to agitate him. His refusal to call her My Lady is the counter-weapon. Two beings who have known each other for three centuries poking at the same bruises they’ve always poked at. “Lucian.” / “You do that deliberately.” / “Yes.”

The Count has done arithmetic that Seraphina has not. He believes — with the confidence of someone who has checked his reasoning from every angle available to a being of his age and intelligence — that the banishment is not permanent. That the door is approaching its opening at or around 1000 AB. He is more afraid than he has been in a very long time, in the specific way of an ancient being who has confirmed that what is coming is larger than even his considerable estimate.

“I see you in the shadows.” — Morgan, four years old, at the edge of a bedroom window.

He has not been the same since. He does not know why, yet. The meaning of that sentence accumulates slowly — across many books, across many years — until when it finally arrives it feels not like a revelation but like a recognition. The reader will have always known.

The True Antagonist

The Being in the Lamp

Its nature is unknown. It predates the Count’s vampirism and bested him in a contest of wits with apparent ease. It was not banished with the Ancient Race — it was imprisoned separately, in the lamp, because it was the source of what they did. Banishment was insufficient for what it is.

Geographically constrained, it has operated exclusively through intermediaries for a thousand years — agents sent into Vaelmere because it cannot come itself. Every move it has made has been through someone else. Its goal is not freedom exactly. It is the completion of what the genocide was supposed to accomplish and didn’t: the permanent prevention of a reunion that would render it irrelevant. A complete humanity doesn’t defeat it. A complete humanity simply has nothing left for it to work with.

The millennium is the threshold it has been working against for a thousand years. It does not change strategy dramatically. It simply persists — with the patience of something that has been imprisoned for a millennium and knows the threshold is coming.

“Not her. There’s still another.”

Aelindra, at the gathering, after naming all the threats she could identify.

She spoke softly. She did not say its name. She may not have known whether saying too much about it aloud was safe.

The Author

B. Aelwyn

B. Aelwyn writes for readers of every age — whimsical picture books for the very young, and the darker novels that share their world. It all takes place in Vaelmere, a realm of ancient forests, dancing northern lights, and creatures made of living light.

The Princess and the Witch began as a bedtime story and grew into a series. The deeper lore — the grown-up history of the same world — is on its way.
